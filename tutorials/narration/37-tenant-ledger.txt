This tutorial reads a tenant's account the way an accountant does, then ties it to the books. As the Accountant, open Tenant Ledger, choose Omar Haddad and the year to 31 August 2026. Miftah shows one block for each account the tenant has touched, and one row for each entry, with a running balance. Start with Rent Receivable. Posting the contract debited 77,000, the rent and the deposit. Each of the five cheques then credited it back, so the balance is zero. That is normal: while the cheques are in hand, the tenant owes nothing on paper. Now choose Layla Nasser. Her second cheque came back from the bank in February. The return debits Rent Receivable 15,000 again. A 500 penalty is added, and its own cheque covers it. Two replacement cheques of 7,500 bring the balance back to zero. Further down, Advance Rent still holds 12,328.76: billed, but not yet earned. PDC Receivable is the paper you hold, and all of Layla's cheques have cleared, so it is zero. Select a document number to open its journal. The return, CBR-26/1, debits Rent Receivable 15,000 and credits PDC Receivable 15,000. Every journal balances, and none is ever edited, only reversed. Now open the General Ledger on Rent Receivable for the Residences, for February. This is the control account: every tenant's entries together. It closes February at 15,000 debit, exactly Layla's balance on that day. The tenant ledgers add up to the control account. Finally, open the Trial Balance as of 31 August 2026. Total debits equal total credits, 4,150,891.24 on each side. Filter to one building, and it still balances.
