This tutorial reviews the accounting structure that connects RentAxis operations to the ledger. Work as an authorised tenant administrator and keep this walkthrough read-only.

In Finance, open Chart of Accounts. The tree view groups the organisation’s accounts into assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and capital. Codes such as A-01, B-01, C-01, and D-01 make the intended type visible before an account is used. Expand a group when you need to inspect its child accounts, or switch to the flat view when a code search is easier. The account count and the Active Only filter help finance confirm the working scope. Add Account and Import Accounts are available for approved changes, but any new code or parent-child relationship should follow the organisation’s accounting design and review process.

Open Account Mappings next. Each event has a debit account for money in or expenses and a credit account for money out or income. The prepared Rent Payment Cleared, Security Deposit Received, Security Deposit Refunded, and Cheque Bounced events show their configured account pairs. An event such as Salary Payment can remain blank when the organisation has not approved a mapping; the page explains that the default mapping will be used. Check that account types match the intended treatment before saving a mapping. Credentials and historical records are not changed by this review.

Finally, open Transactions to see the downstream effect. Rental-income rows, cheque-bounce entries, and split expense transactions carry dates, descriptions, property or organisation scope, and money-in or money-out totals. A mapping controls future postings; it should never be used to reinterpret an existing journal without an approved adjustment process. Reconcile the account hierarchy, mappings, and transaction descriptions together before enabling live finance operations.
