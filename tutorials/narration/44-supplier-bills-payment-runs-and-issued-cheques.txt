This tutorial covers the money going out: supplier bills, paying them, and supplier cheques. As the Accountant, open Receipt and Payment Vouchers. Every supplier bill and every payment of the year is listed here. Create a Purchase Invoice from BlueWave Cleaning for September's common-area cleaning at Palm Ridge Residences. Enter BlueWave's invoice number and date. The due date follows their 30-day terms. The line goes to Cleaning Services: 2,400, plus 5 percent VAT of 120, a total of 2,520. Post it, then open its journal from the voucher list. The purchase invoice journal debits Cleaning Services with 2,400 and Input VAT with 120, and credits BlueWave with 2,520, the amount you owe. Input VAT is VAT you can reclaim on your VAT return. Now open Payables aging. BlueWave's 2,520 sits in Current, because it is not due yet. The last column checks that each supplier's open invoices add up to their balance in the ledger, and here the difference is zero. To pay, open Payment runs and start a new run. List what is due by 31 October and tick BlueWave's invoice. Pay by transfer on 30 September, from the Emirates Islamic account for the Residences. The preview shows the payment voucher before anything is written: BlueWave debited, the bank credited, 2,520. It also warns that BlueWave has no bank account number on file for the bank upload file. Post the run. Each supplier gets one payment voucher, and if anything fails, nothing is posted. When you pay by a cheque dated later, the bank has not paid yet, so the voucher credits PDC Payable, issued cheques, instead of the bank. Open Issued cheques. BlueWave's August cheque, 004501, was presented on 12 September. Presenting it posted a journal that debits PDC Payable and credits the bank: the bank balance falls when the bank pays, not when you write the cheque. Cheque 004521 is dated 20 October and still outstanding, and the outstanding total equals the PDC Payable balance.
