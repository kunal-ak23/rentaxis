This tutorial covers the money going out: supplier bills, paying them, and supplier cheques. Open Receipt and Payment Vouchers and create a Purchase Invoice from BlueWave Cleaning for August: 2,400 for common-area cleaning at Palm Ridge Residences, plus 5 percent VAT. Enter BlueWave's invoice number and date. The due date follows their 30-day terms. Post it. The purchase invoice journal debits Cleaning Services with 2,400 and Input VAT with 120, and credits BlueWave with 2,520, the amount you owe. Input VAT is VAT you can reclaim, so Miftah will not post it unless the supplier's tax registration number is on file. Now open Payables aging. Every open supplier invoice sits in a column by how overdue it is. The last column checks that each supplier's open invoices add up to their balance in the ledger, and here the difference is zero. To pay, open Payment runs and start a new run. Choose the date, the bank account and the method, and Miftah lists every invoice due by then. Tick what you are paying. The preview shows, for each supplier, the payment voucher it will post and any problem, such as a missing bank account number. Post it. Each supplier gets one payment voucher that debits their account and credits the bank, and their invoices now show as Paid. Download the bank upload file for your bank. When you pay by a cheque dated later, the bank has not paid yet. The August run was paid by cheques dated 10 September, so its vouchers credited PDC Payable, issued cheques, not the bank. Open Issued cheques. When the bank paid them on 12 September, marking them Presented posted a journal that debits PDC Payable and credits the bank. The bank balance falls when the bank pays, not when you write the cheque. The September run's cheques are dated 20 October and are still outstanding, and their total equals the PDC Payable balance.
