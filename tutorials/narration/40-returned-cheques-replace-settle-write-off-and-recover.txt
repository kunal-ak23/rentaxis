A cheque the bank returns, a bounced cheque, can end in three ways. This tutorial follows each one through the books, on cases from Palm Ridge's past year. As the Accountant, open Cheque and Cash Collection and search the register for Layla Nasser. Her cheque 220102 for 15,000 came back unpaid in February and now shows Replaced: she gave two cheques of 7,500, and both have cleared. The 500 cash row is the cheque return penalty. Open her Tenant Ledger to see it in the books. The return journal debited Rent Receivable 15,000 and credited PDC Receivable: the cheque no longer covered the debt, so Layla owed it again. A 500 penalty was added and paid. Each replacement wrote its own post-dated cheque journal, crediting Rent Receivable with 7,500, so the balance came back to zero. Had the replacements come to less, the shortfall would have stayed here as money still owed. Arjun Mehta settled his returned cheque 330103 in cash at the office. In the register that is a replacement row with the mode Cash, received on the spot: it debited the Cash Account and credited PDC Receivable with 14,000, and a journal voucher later moved the cash to the bank. Some debts are never paid. Daniel Brooks left after his December cheque bounced. His contract was terminated and settled against his deposit, and 11,326.03 was still unpaid. On his contract, More actions, Write off shows the bad debt, written off with a reason and approved by the Company Admin. Approval posted one journal, debiting Bad debts written off and crediting Rent Receivable, and took the debt off every list of money due. In July a collection agency recovered 5,000. The recovery journal debited the bank and credited Bad debts recovered, which counts as other income. The write-off stays, so the history is honest. Back on Cheque and Cash Collection, Daniel's cheque is neither due nor waiting for a replacement.
