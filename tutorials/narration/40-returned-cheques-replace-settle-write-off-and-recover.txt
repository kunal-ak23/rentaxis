A cheque the bank returns, also called a bounced cheque, can end in three ways, and this tutorial shows each one in the books. Open Cheque and Cash Collection and filter to Bounced. Layla Nasser's cheque 220102 for 15,000 came back unpaid. Open it. The return journal debits Rent Receivable and credits PDC Receivable with 15,000. The cheque no longer covers the debt, so Layla owes the amount again, and the register lists it as due. Layla replaced it with two cheques of 7,500. Choose Return and replace and add the two rows, each with its own number and date. Each replacement writes its own post-dated cheque journal, debiting PDC Receivable and crediting Rent Receivable. If the replacements came to less than the returned cheque, the shortfall would stay in Rent Receivable as money still owed. Arjun Mehta paid his returned cheque 330103 in cash at the office. That is a replacement row with the mode Cash, received on the spot. Receiving it debits the Cash Account and credits PDC Receivable with 14,000. Two days later the cash was banked with a journal voucher that moves it from the Cash Account to the bank. Some debts are not paid. Daniel Brooks left after his December cheque bounced. His contract was terminated and settled against his deposit, and the balance was still unpaid. Open his contract and choose Write off. Miftah lists the open items. Propose the write-off with a reason, and the Company Admin approves it. Approval posts one bad debt journal, debiting Bad debts written off and crediting Rent Receivable, and takes the items off every list of money due. In July a collection agency recovered 5,000. Select Record recovery, choose the bank account and the date. The recovery journal debits the bank and credits Bad debts recovered, which counts as other income. The write-off is not undone, so the history stays accurate. Open Profit and Loss to see the bad debt as an expense and the recovery as income.
