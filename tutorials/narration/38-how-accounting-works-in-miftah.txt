This tutorial follows one tenancy contract's money through Miftah's books, from posting to the reports. As the Accountant, open Omar Haddad's contract for unit R-101 at Palm Ridge Residences: a 5,000 security deposit, and 72,000 in rent for the year. Select View posting journal. Posting wrote one tenancy contract journal, TCO. It debits Rent Receivable with 5,000 and 72,000: the 77,000 Omar has agreed to pay. It credits Security Deposit with 5,000, because the deposit is held for Omar and is not income. And it credits Advance Rent with 72,000, because the rent is billed now but not yet earned. Back on the contract, open the Cheques tab. Omar handed over five post-dated cheques. Journal Vouchers lists every journal the contract wrote. After the TCO come five post-dated cheque journals, PDR. In the Rent Receivable ledger below, each one credits Rent Receivable against PDC Receivable, and the balance falls to zero: with the cheques in hand, Omar owes nothing on paper. Cheque 110102 cleared in January. Banking a cheque writes nothing. Clearing writes a receipt journal, CRT: it debits the Emirates Islamic account for Palm Ridge Residences with 18,000, and credits PDC Receivable. The money is the company's when the bank pays it. Now open Revenue recognition. The rent is spread over the term by the day: October's 31 days earn 6,115.07, and November's 30 days earn 5,917.81. October's journal debits Advance Rent and credits Rental Income with 6,115.07. Income is earned day by day, whenever the cash arrives. Next, a cheque that went wrong. Open the Tenant Ledger for Layla Nasser. Her rent receivable was at zero until the bank returned cheque 220102 in February, and 15,000 came back. Two replacement cheques of 7,500 closed it again. Open the return journal, CBR: it debits Rent Receivable and credits PDC Receivable with 15,000, so the debt is back on the tenant. In the Trial Balance, the grand total of the debits equals the credits. Profit and Loss shows the rental income earned in August, not the cash collected. One ledger feeds every one of these screens.
