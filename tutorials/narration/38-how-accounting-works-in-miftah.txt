This tutorial follows one tenancy contract's money through Miftah's books, from the day it is posted to the reports. Open Omar Haddad's contract for unit R-101 at Palm Ridge Residences: 72,000 in rent for a year, and a 5,000 security deposit. Open the Journals tab. Posting the contract wrote one tenancy contract journal. It debits Rent Receivable with 77,000, which is what Omar has agreed to pay. It credits Advance Rent with 72,000, because rent is billed now but not yet earned, and it credits Security Deposit with 5,000, because the deposit is held for Omar and is not income. Below it are five post-dated cheque journals, one for each cheque. Each one debits PDC Receivable and credits Rent Receivable, so once the cheques are in hand, Omar owes nothing on paper. Now open Cheque and Cash Collection and find cheque 110102. Taking it to the bank writes nothing. When the bank confirms it, the clearing journal debits the Emirates Islamic account for Palm Ridge Residences with 18,000 and credits PDC Receivable. The money is the company's when the bank pays it, not when the cheque is handed over. Back on the contract, open the Recognition schedule tab. Miftah divides the rent by the 365 days of the term, which gives 197.26 a day, and multiplies it by the days in each month. October has 31 days, so 6,115.07 moves from Advance Rent into Rental Income at the end of October. That is income earned day by day, whenever the cash arrives. Now look at a cheque that went wrong: Layla Nasser's cheque 220102, returned by the bank. The return journal debits Rent Receivable and credits PDC Receivable with 15,000, so the debt is back on Layla. Open the Tenant Ledger for Layla and you see that 15,000 reopened. Open the Trial Balance: total debits equal total credits. Open Profit and Loss: it shows rental income earned, not cash collected. One ledger feeds every one of these screens.
