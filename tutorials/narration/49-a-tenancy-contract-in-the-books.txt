This tutorial follows the contract from tutorial 47 into the books. Open Rajesh Kumar's contract for A-202, and select View posting journal. Posting wrote journal TCO-26/7. Rent Receivable is debited with each part of the contract, 47,000 in all: what Rajesh owes. Against it, the rent of 42,000 is credited to Advance Rent, not to income, because rent is earned day by day over the term. The deposit of 4,000 is credited to Security Deposit: it is held for Rajesh, and is never income. The admin fee of 1,000 is income the day it is charged. Back on the contract, the Cheques tab lists every journal it has written, under Journal Vouchers. Posting also wrote one PDR for each of the six cheques in hand. Each moves its amount from Rent Receivable to PDC Receivable: the cheques the landlord holds. Open CRT-26/12. Clearing the deposit cheque wrote CRT-26/12. The building's bank account is debited 4,000, and PDC Receivable is credited the same: the cheque is now money in the bank. Below the list are the same entries, account by account. Advance Rent holds the full 42,000, none of it earned yet. The bank holds the 4,000 deposit. PDC Receivable holds 43,000: the cheques still to clear. And Rent Receivable is back to zero: with every cheque in hand, Rajesh owes nothing on account. Revenue recognition shows how the rent will be earned: twelve monthly slices, worked out by the day. At each month-end, that month's slice moves from Advance Rent to Rental Income: 3,452.05 for the thirty days of November. The last month absorbs the rounding, and the schedule matches the contract rent of 42,000. Nobody posts these by hand: every night, each month that has ended is posted on its own, and its row turns from Planned to Posted, with its journal. Tutorial 51 shows it happening on these contracts, and how to check that nothing was missed.
