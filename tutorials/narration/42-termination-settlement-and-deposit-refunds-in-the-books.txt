This tutorial ends a tenancy contract early and follows every entry through to the deposit refund. Grace Okafor rented R-203 for 70,000 a year from 15 October 2025. She relocated and left on 31 May 2026. Open her contract and choose Terminate, with 31 May as the date. The preview lists her cheques. Three rent cheques have cleared. The July cheque is dated after she leaves, so it is returned to her by default. Returning it reverses its post-dated cheque journal, so PDC Receivable no longer holds it. Confirm. Miftah now works out what Grace actually earned for the landlord: 229 days at the daily rate, 43,917.81. The rest of the year's rent was never earned, so a reversal journal debits Advance Rent and credits Rent Receivable with 26,082.19. Grace paid 52,500, which is 8,582.19 more than she used, so her Rent Receivable balance is now a credit: she has paid in advance. Open the Settlement. The statement lists the rent earned, the money received, the 3,500 deposit held, and a deduction of 750 for move-out cleaning. The refund due is 11,332.19. Select Finalise. The settlement journal releases the deposit by debiting Security Deposit, clears her prepaid balance by debiting Rent Receivable, credits Maintenance Charges with the 750 for cleaning, and credits Refunds payable with 11,332.19. The refund is owed to Grace until it is paid, so it sits as a liability. Five days later it was paid by transfer. Open Receipt and Payment Vouchers and choose a payment voucher against the settlement. It debits Refunds payable and credits the Residences bank account with 11,332.19, and the settlement shows the refund as paid. When a deposit is not enough, as with Daniel Brooks in R-104, the balance due stays in Rent Receivable, and Miftah adds a row to the collection register to collect it. The returned cheques tutorial shows how that debt was later written off.
