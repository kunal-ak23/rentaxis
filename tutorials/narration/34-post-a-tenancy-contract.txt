This tutorial posts a tenancy contract, which writes its accounting entries and makes it active. As a Company Admin, open Leasing. The pinned view Contracts to post lists the contracts still waiting for this step. Open unit M-1501, Sara Mansour's contract. Its status is Draft, so nothing has reached the ledger yet, and it can still be edited. It has two charge lines, each credited to its own account. The security deposit is held for the tenant, not income, so it goes to the Security Deposit account. The rent goes to Advance Rent, and it is earned over the term. Open the Cheques tab. The grid holds the five post-dated cheques Sara handed over: the deposit, and four rent instalments. Their total must equal the contract value of 140,000, and the grid confirms that it does. If it did not, the contract could not be posted. Select Post Contract. Review before posting runs every check a post would run, and writes nothing. It shows the contract value, the VAT, and the cheque total, and it lists what posting will write: one tenancy contract journal, and one post-dated cheque journal for each of the five cheques. It reports Ready to post, so select Post Contract. The contract is now Active, and the banner names the journal it wrote. In the Cheques tab, open Journal Vouchers to see all six. Then open Revenue recognition. The rent is already planned across the term, one row per month, each using its real number of days. Every row is Planned, and the total matches the contract rent. Once posted, a contract is not edited. Changes are made with actions such as Amend Lines, Extend Contract, or Add charge, and each writes its own entries.
