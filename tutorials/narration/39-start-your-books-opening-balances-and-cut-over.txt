This tutorial shows how an organisation starts its books in Miftah when it moves from another system. Signed in as the Company Admin, open Accounting and choose Year End Closing. Palm Ridge's books start on 1 September 2025, and its fiscal year starts in January. The period before the books start is locked, so nothing can be posted into the months your old system still owns. From here on, the Accountant takes over. Open Import Batches under One-time setup. Contracts that are still running come across from the cut-over workbook, with their outstanding cheques. Posting a batch writes each contract's journals on its own dates, and a batch can be reversed as a whole while you check it. Palm Ridge's contracts were entered one by one, so there are no batches here. Now open Opening Balances. The accounts that contracts produce, such as rent receivable, post-dated cheques, advance rent and deposits, are greyed out: they are worked out from the contracts, and you never type them. Everything else is entered as it stood on 31 August 2025: 250,000 in the Residences bank account, 180,000 in the Business Centre account, 12,600 owed to Coastline Facility Services, and 417,400 of capital. Debits and credits both total 430,000, so the difference is zero. Opening balances are the last step: once they are posted, an import batch cannot be posted or reversed until they are reversed first. In Journal Voucher, the opening balance journal, OB, debits the two bank accounts with 250,000 and 180,000, and credits Coastline with 12,600 and Capital with 417,400. The books open balanced. The 12,600 owed to Coastline is one real invoice, so it is recorded under AP opening items, with its number and due date. It was paid in October's payment run, and the check shows the supplier's open items agreeing with its opening balance, with no difference. Finally, open Cut-over reconciliation. For every account it compares Miftah's balance, leaving out the opening journal, with the old system's trial balance. The four rows that differ are exactly the ones entered by hand; any account worked out from contracts must show no difference, and the total is zero.
