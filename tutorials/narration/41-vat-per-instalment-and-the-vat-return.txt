This tutorial follows 5 percent VAT on a commercial tenancy contract from posting to the VAT return. Open Noor Pharmacy's draft contract for shop BC-G01: 150,000 in rent plus VAT, and a 15,000 security deposit. Post it. The contract journal debits Rent Receivable and credits Advance Rent with 150,000 and Security Deposit with 15,000. The VAT, 7,500, is debited to Rent Receivable and credited to an account called Output VAT not yet due. A tenancy contract is not a tax invoice, so posting it does not make the VAT payable. Open the Cheques tab. Each of the four rent cheques of 39,375 carries 1,875 of VAT in its VAT column, and the deposit carries none. Each instalment brings its own VAT with it. Now open Meridian Logistics, whose contract has run for a year, and open the VAT schedule tab. There are four tax points, on 1 October, 1 January, 1 April and 1 July. A tax point is the date the VAT becomes due: the instalment's due date, or the day it was paid if that came first. On each one Miftah posted a journal that debits Output VAT not yet due and credits Output VAT with 1,500. Open Final Reports and choose VAT return, then pick the quarter starting in July 2026. Select Run tax points to date so that every instalment due in the quarter is included. Box 1 shows the standard-rated rent and its VAT, and box 9 shows the input VAT you paid on supplier bills. The output check confirms that box 1 agrees with the Output VAT account. Select any box to see the contracts and bills behind it. When the figures agree with what you submit to the Federal Tax Authority, the Company Admin selects File and enters the filing reference. Filing locks the quarter: from then on, anything that touches VAT in that quarter is refused, and a correction goes into the next open quarter. The contract's Documents tab holds the tax invoices the tenant receives.
