This tutorial follows 5 percent VAT on a commercial tenancy contract, from posting to the VAT return. As the Accountant, open Noor Pharmacy's draft contract for shop BC-G01 at Palm Ridge Business Centre: 150,000 in rent plus 7,500 of VAT, and a 15,000 security deposit. Select Post Contract, check the review, and post it. Now select View posting journal. The deposit and the rent are debited to Rent Receivable and credited to Security Deposit and Advance Rent. The VAT is its own pair: Rent Receivable is debited with 7,500, and Output VAT not yet due is credited. A tenancy contract is not a tax invoice, so posting it does not make the VAT payable. Back on the contract, open the Cheques tab. Each of the four rent cheques of 39,375 carries 1,875 of VAT, and the deposit carries none. Each instalment brings its own VAT. Now open Meridian Logistics' contract, which has run for a year, and its VAT section. There are four tax points: 1 October, 1 January, 1 April and 1 July, each with its own numbered tax invoice. A tax point is the instalment's due date, or the day it was paid if that came first. Open the July journal: it debits Output VAT not yet due and credits Output VAT on Sales with 1,500. Tax points are declared every night, and on Month-end recognition you can declare them yourself before closing a period. Preview shows what has fallen due; here nothing is waiting, because every tax point to date is declared. Now open Final Reports, VAT return, and pick the quarter from July 2026. Box 1b holds the standard-rated rent in Dubai and its VAT, and box 9 the input VAT on supplier bills. The check confirms that the output VAT ties to the ledger. Select box 1b to see the documents behind it. The quarter ends on 30 September, and only then can it be filed. The April quarter shows a filed return: the Company Admin filed it with the tax authority's reference, and filing locks VAT dated in that quarter.
