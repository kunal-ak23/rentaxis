This tutorial shows how Miftah's chart of accounts is organised, how every property gets its own set of accounts, and which account each charge on a contract posts to. As the Accountant, open Accounting and choose Chart of Accounts. The tree groups every account under five types: assets, liabilities, income, expenses and equity. Groups such as Rental Receivable and Bank only hold other accounts. The accounts beneath them, the leaves, are where journals post, and a balance is always the sum of its journal lines, never a typed figure. Filter by property and choose Palm Ridge Residences. Now you see that building's own accounts: its Rent Receivable, PDC Receivable, Emirates Islamic bank account, Advance Rent, Security Deposit and Rental Income. Each building keeps its own books inside the organisation. Open the property itself, Palm Ridge Residences, and its Ledger accounts tab. Every role, such as Rent Receivable or Advance Rent, points to one account for this property, and Change maps a role to a different one. Posting rules only name roles; the property decides the account. Next, open Property account template under One-time setup. For every role there is a name pattern, where the placeholder is replaced by the property's name, and the parent group the new account files under. When a building is added, its account set is created from this template automatically. Further down, Default accounts are the company-wide accounts that are not per building: the Cash Account, Output VAT on Sales, Input VAT on Purchases, PDC Payable for issued cheques, and bank charges and interest. Finally, open Charge types. These are the charges a contract can carry. Rent is earned day by day over the term, and a security deposit is held for the tenant, never income. For each fee you choose how it is earned: the Parking Fee over the term, like rent; the Admin Fee and the Renewal Fee once, when charged; and Utilities recovered at cost, not income. The charge type decides where each contract line is credited.
