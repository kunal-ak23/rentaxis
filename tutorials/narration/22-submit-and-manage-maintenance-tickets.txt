This tutorial reviews a maintenance request from the property team’s perspective. Use the prepared Tutorial Studio tenant and keep the walkthrough read-only.

The Maintenance Tickets queue shows the ticket reference, title, property, unit, priority, status, assignee, creation date, and the View action. Use the search box and status or priority filters to locate work that needs attention. Create Ticket is available for an approved request, but the queue is also the safest place to confirm that the request belongs to the right property and unit before changing it.

Open the prepared leaking-kitchen-tap ticket. Its detail keeps the renter’s description, category, priority, property, unit, reporter, assignee, and creation date together. The attachment area shows whether diagnostic photos or files were supplied. The conversation records the renter’s access note and provides a reply box for the next update. Assign work only after reviewing the description; then record the owner, estimate, expected arrival, replies, and status transitions in the ticket’s supported controls. Move to In Progress only when work has started, and use Resolved or Closed only when the technician and renter workflow allow it. Never submit the same transition twice while a request is processing.

Finish on Ticket Reports. The summary shows total and open tickets, average resolution and satisfaction when available, and breakdowns by status, category, and priority. Here the prepared ticket is open, high priority, and plumbing, so the report remains consistent with the queue and detail page. A reliable maintenance record preserves the full conversation and explains what was reported, who owns the work, when status changed, and whether the outcome was confirmed.
