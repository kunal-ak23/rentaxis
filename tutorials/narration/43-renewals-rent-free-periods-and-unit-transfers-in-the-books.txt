This tutorial covers three changes to a tenancy contract, and what each one writes in the books. First, a renewal. As the Company Admin, open Omar Haddad's contract for R-101, which ends on 30 September, and choose Renew. The new term runs from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027. Choose By percent and enter 5: the rent moves from 72,000 to 75,600. Keep Carry deposit forward, add a Renewal Fee of 525, and select Renew. The renewal is a new draft contract, with its own cheques and its own posting journal. Priya Raman's contract shows the finished result. Its header reads Rent revised from 64,000 to 67,200, plus 5 percent, and links back to the contract it renewed. The Renewal Fee is income when charged, and it is not copied at the next renewal. In its journals, the deposit carried forward is a journal voucher: the deposit never left, so 3,200 moves from the old contract's Security Deposit to the new one's. Second, a rent-free period. Sahara Design Studio took office BC-302 at 96,000 a year, with February free for their fit-out. The concession is 7,364.38, so the rent line posts 88,635.62, and VAT of 4,431.78 is charged on that lower figure. Income is spread evenly over the whole term, the free month included. Third, a unit transfer. Open Mohammed Al Rashid's contract for R-202 and choose Transfer. He moves to R-203 after the night of 30 September. The preview lists his cheques, each to carry to the new contract, keep or return. It shows what he has earned on the current unit, the 13,264.93 he has paid ahead, which moves to the new contract, and his 3,900 deposit, carried across. There is no gap to collect, and nothing is posted until the new contract is.
