This tutorial covers three changes to a tenancy contract and what each one writes in the books. First, a renewal. Omar Haddad's contract for R-101 ends on 30 September. Open it and choose Renew. Set the new term, choose By percent and enter 5, and the rent moves from 72,000 to 75,600. Keep Carry deposit forward selected and add the renewal fee. The renewal is a new contract with its own posting journal and its own cheques, and when it is posted the old contract shows as Renewed. The deposit never leaves the landlord's hands, so a journal voucher simply moves 5,000 from the old contract's security deposit to the new one. Priya Raman's contract shows the result of the same steps. Its header reads Rent revised from 64,000 to 67,200, plus 5 percent. The renewal fee is a one-off charge, so it will not be copied next year. Second, a rent-free period. Sahara Design Studio took office BC-302 for a year at 96,000, with February free for their fit-out. The free month is worth 96,000 times 28 days divided by 365, which is 7,364.38. So the contract posts 88,635.62 to Advance Rent, and VAT is charged on that lower figure. On the Recognition schedule tab, February is greyed out: a free month earns nothing. The rest of the term is recognised by the day. Third, a unit transfer. Mohammed Al Rashid wants to move from R-202 to the larger R-203. Choose Transfer and read the preview. His uncleared cheques move to the new contract as they are, so he does not write new ones. The old contract is cut off on the move date, like a termination. Whatever he has prepaid or still owes is carried across by a journal voucher, and so is his deposit. After the transfer, both contracts balance to zero. Open the History strip on the new contract: it links each contract in the chain, marked Renewed or Transferred, so the tenant's whole story stays in one place.
